The true values are in each brief's truth folder; the capstone is assessed on the chain and the defence, not on recovering them. Each brief has one district or office with poor overlap in the logs: a README that values a rule there without diagnostics loses marks under offline evaluation. In the Siraj brief the effect is largest in old homes and high-use homes, so need targeting by consumption is a reasonable but not the best rule; in Nakheel, restaurants with the worst records are not the ones inspections change most.

The submission

The README reports measured results, in this order

  1. The estimand, and the average effect with its overlap
  2. Effect estimates validated on the held-out randomised split
  3. A rule under the budget and the constraint, with the attribute register
  4. Its offline value with intervals and diagnostics, against treat-all, risk or need targeting and current practice
  5. The audit by the brief's groups, and the constraint's price
  6. The confirmatory trial: arms, difference, seekers or units per arm, stopping rule

And with it

Notebooks that rerun from the raw data with a fixed seed, the commit history, the run log, and a verification note on every assisted step.

AI-full in the capstone: participants may use assistants for anything, and the verification note says what was assisted and how each result was checked. The live change is how the panel checks that the participant understands what the code does.

How evaluations are scored

The rubric

Identification
15%
Effects and validation
25%
Offline evaluation
25%
Rule and fairness audit
20%
Trial plan and reproducibility
15%
Every number reruns. A number the panel cannot regenerate from the repository scores as a claim, not a result.

Full marks, briefly. Identification: estimand in five parts, overlap shown and acted on. Effects and validation: at least two estimators, calibration and a best-linear-predictor slope on the held-out split. Offline evaluation: augmented values with intervals, effective sample sizes, the recorded probabilities checked. Rule and fairness audit: a register with reasons, selection rates and gains by group, the constraint priced with a paired interval. Trial plan and reproducibility: dilution, unit, stopping rule, limits; a clean clone reruns.

Format

Fifteen minutes: the evaluation, then one live change

1 · The question

Who decides, the budget, the estimand. One slide.

2 · The evidence

Effects validated, the rule, its value against the alternatives, the audit.

3 · The live change

The panel changes the budget or the constraint; predict, then rerun.

Peer feedback. After each defence, everyone writes one strength, one question and one suggestion.

Call the live change at about minute ten, whatever has been covered; what was not said is not scored.

What the panel changes

One change to the budget or the constraint, predicted before it is rerun

The change

Halve the budget, double it, or remove the fairness constraint. The participant first predicts the direction and rough size of the change in the rule's value and in the audit, then reruns and shows it.

  • "With half the places, does the value per place rise or fall? Why?"
  • "Without the constraint, which group loses places, and how many outcomes do you gain?"
  • "What is the effective sample size of your rule's weights now?"
  • "How many units per arm would the trial need at the new budget?"

Use the same change for everyone on the same brief so defences are comparable. A good prediction: halving the budget raises the gain per place (the best places are kept) and lowers the total; removing the constraint raises the value by about the constraint's price, which may be zero within its interval. A participant who cannot rerun within two minutes has a reproducibility problem.
