# Follow-up task: review one eligibility rule

A tracked task, due within three weeks of the course. You take one rule that decides who receives a service, a place or an inspection, in your workplace or in a public service, and review it the way the course reviewed Fursa's options. You do not need record-level data and you do not need to run an analysis. It takes about three hours, including a conversation with the rule's owner.

## What to choose

A rule that allocates something scarce: who is offered a training place, which accounts are reviewed first, which sites are inspected, who is eligible for a support payment. It may be a written threshold ("unemployed more than six months"), a score with a cut-off or a caseworker's discretion. Never copy personal data, record-level extracts or confidential figures into the review: describe the data, do not include them.

## The review

At most one page, in seven parts.

1. **The rule** in one sentence, its owner and its budget (how many receive it, out of how many).
2. **Risk or benefit?** Does the rule target those most in need, or those the service would help most? Say what evidence exists for either.
3. **The evidence of effect**: was there ever a randomised pilot, a natural experiment or logged decisions with recorded probabilities? If not, what would it take to create one?
4. **The attribute register**: every attribute the rule uses or could use, each marked drive, audit or exclude, with a reason. For personal data, name the instruments that set the conditions (for example the Personal Data Protection Law); do not paraphrase their articles from memory.
5. **The audit you would run**: the groups, the metric (selection rate, and gain per unit served where it can be estimated) and the threshold that would trigger a change.
6. **Edge cases**: at least three kinds of case the rule decides badly, and who should review them.
7. **What would confirm a better rule**: the arms of a trial, the difference to detect after dilution, and the randomisation unit.

## The conversation

Show the review to the rule's owner and record, in three lines at the end: what they agreed with, what they questioned, and one change they would consider.

## What to submit

A Markdown file of at most 900 words: the seven parts and the three lines. Add it to your capstone repository as `followup.md`, or submit it as your trainer directs.

## How it is read

The trainer reads for the use of the course's ideas: risk separated from benefit, a register with reasons, an audit with a metric and a threshold, edge cases with a reviewer, and a trial whose size accounts for dilution. A review that concludes the rule should stay as it is is acceptable when the reasons are given part by part.
